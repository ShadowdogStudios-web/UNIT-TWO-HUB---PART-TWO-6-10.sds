The Vote — and Its Limits

A right extended to some raises a hard question about the rest. Read the perspectives and judge. Your work saves automatically on this computer.

Source · the 15th Amendment · 1870

The text

“The right of citizens of the United States to vote shall not be denied or abridged … on account of race, color, or previous condition of servitude.” — U.S. Constitution, Amendment XV. Public domain.

The image · 1867

“The First Vote”

“The First Vote” — wood engraving by Alfred R. Waud, Harper's Weekly, 1867, public domain (Library of Congress).

Step 2 · Who was left out

Read the words carefully

Question 2

Step 3 · Two points of view

A painful split

Question 3

Step 4 · The loophole

Why it matters

Question 4
The amendment banned denying the vote “on account of race” — but not other excuses. In three or four sentences, explain how Southern states later used poll taxes and literacy tests to get around it, and why the wording made that possible.
