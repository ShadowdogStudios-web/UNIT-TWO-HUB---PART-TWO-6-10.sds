The Compromises
That Failed

1820 — 1854

For a generation, every time slavery threatened to split the country in two, Congress struck a bargain to hold it together.

  • 1820
    The Missouri Compromise. Congress drew a line straight across the map. Slavery could spread below it; above it the territories would be free.
  • 1850
    The Compromise of 1850. A new set of bargains kept the peace one more time — but it came with a harsh Fugitive Slave Act that reached into free states.
  • cost
    Each deal bought a few more years. None of them settled the question. They only pushed it down the road.

By 1854, there was nothing left to trade.

The broken line, 1854 — illustration, © ShadowDog Digital Studios (SDS Digital Collection).

One law tore the last line in two. The Kansas–Nebraska Act threw the question open to a vote — and undid thirty years of bargains in a single stroke. 1854
